# Trap types

Each: name, meaning, sample user reason, uncle's line.

1. Gambler's fallacy: past results change the odds. "It dropped 30%, it's due to bounce." -> "Black is due!"
2. Hot hand: a streak will keep going. "It went up all week." -> "I'm on fire tonight!"
3. Herd thinking: buying because others are. "Everyone's buying it." -> "The whole table's on 17!"
4. Hot tip: a source you can't check. "I saw it on TikTok." -> "A fella at the shop told me."
5. Gut feeling: a hunch, no evidence. "I've got a good feeling." -> "My lucky shirt's on."
6. Loss chasing: adding money because of a loss, with no new reason the asset is worth more. "I'll average down and get even." -> "One more spin and I'm even."
7. Anchoring: judging anything about the asset only against its own past, instead of against what it is worth now. "It used to be 100, so it's cheap." -> "It used to pay 100 baht!"
8. Overconfidence: too clever to lose. "I know this market." -> "I've got a system, mate."
9. Fear of missing out: rushing before it's too late. "I have to get in now." -> "Quick, the wheel's about to spin!"
10. Story over numbers: a nice story, no facts. "AI is the future." -> "This table's got a good vibe."

Roulette test for anything else: would this reason work just as well at a roulette table? If yes, it's a gamble.
